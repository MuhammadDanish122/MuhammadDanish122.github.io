Architecture showcase

The CDC design I run in production

A cleaned-up reference version of the platform I designed and run: 200+ tables, peaks above 28k rows a minute, and no skipped events. Client names and internals are left out on purpose.

reference architecture · OLTP → OLAP + webhookslive flow
01

Fail-closed by default

A consumer writes a batch, waits for the warehouse to confirm, and only then commits the offset. A bad message stops that table and raises an alert. It never gets skipped quietly.

02

One stream, two jobs

A separate consumer group reads the same topics for customer webhooks. Lookups against serving tables are batched, so webhooks and analytics fail independently.

03

History without double counting

Backfills run in their own lane with batch inserts and a hard-delete path, so you can load years of history while live capture keeps going.

04

Built to be operated

A control plane reconciles desired state, starts and stops consumers, and handles Active–Standby failover. Another engineer can run it at 2am.

FAQ

Questions people ask before hiring me

What kind of work do you take on?

Data platforms end to end: real-time CDC from MySQL or Postgres, warehouse builds and migrations (ClickHouse, Snowflake, Microsoft Fabric, dbt with Iceberg or Delta), pipeline audits, and reporting in Power BI or Looker. Fixed-scope packages or a monthly retainer.

How do your prices work?

Every package has a fixed price and a fixed scope, written down before work starts. The prices come from my hourly rate of $60 and the hours a package normally takes. If something bigger turns up, I tell you and quote it separately. Nothing gets added to the bill quietly.

Where are you based, and what hours do you work?

I work remotely from Pakistan (UTC+5). That gives me a good overlap with European working hours and with US mornings. I studied in Finland and have worked with teams in Estonia and Finland.

Do you work through Upwork or directly?

Either. I'm on Upwork as Danish R., and I'm happy to work on a direct contract and invoice if you prefer. Email me and we'll pick whichever is easier for you.

Will you need access to production?

Only as much as the job needs, and I ask for read-only access first. Changes to production go through a dry run and your sign-off. That's how I run my own platform.

What happens after handover?

You get the code in your repository, a runbook, architecture notes and a recorded walkthrough. If you want me around after that, the fractional architect retainer covers it. Otherwise your team owns it.

Got data you don't fully trust?

Tell me what's in it, where it goes and what's going wrong. You'll get a straight answer on whether I can help and what it would cost.
